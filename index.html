About

I spent a decade writing production software. Now I build the AI and data systems that keep businesses running on it.

I'm Harrison Smith, an AI and data engineer based in Ogden, Utah. My background is classic software engineering: backend services, SQL systems, CI/CD pipelines. The last several years, I've rebuilt that foundation around AI, with RAG pipelines, multi-model orchestration across Claude, Gemini, and ChatGPT, local LLM hosting, and the data engineering that makes any of it trustworthy.

Day to day, I'm a Business Intelligence Analyst at a venture-backed payments company, where I build the BigQuery models and dashboards Finance and Risk run on, and own the evaluation framework for their production AI agent. I bring that same production discipline to independent contract engineering through HSmithDev LLC.

That role is also where the reporting platform below comes from: 15 internal BI apps, replacing a legacy Tableau stack, built with Claude Code, Grok, and Codex on top of a data warehouse I know end to end. I've also contributed training data and review to the company's internal AI agent, which gives those apps natural-language context about that warehouse.

My M.S. in Applied AI pushed me deep into the parts a bootcamp skips: model training and evaluation, ETL design, and the statistics behind knowing whether a model actually works. I build with Claude Code as a first-class part of my workflow, to move faster through engineering, not skip it.

How I work.

  1. Scope

    I start from the actual requirements, not a generic discovery deck: scope the system, flag the risks, and lock a plan before any code gets written.

  2. Build

    I build in the open: working increments, real commits, and Claude Code as a first-class part of the workflow, not a replacement for engineering judgment.

  3. Ship

    Production means production: CI/CD, monitoring, and documentation, not a demo that only works on my laptop.

  4. Support

    I stay reachable after launch. On contract work that means a support window; on a team it means I don't disappear once something ships.
